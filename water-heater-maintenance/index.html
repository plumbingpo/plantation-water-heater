Two quiet processes decide how long a water heater lasts: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they steal efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that gives itself up so the tank lining is spared. Nobody sees either process happening, and by the time a homeowner notices, the damage is usually done. That is the reason maintenance matters for Plantation, FL homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

Our maintenance visit is built to interrupt both problems. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, pull and inspect the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we test the elements and thermostats with a meter and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, verify the temperature setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

We maintain tankless units as well. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that restrict flow and reduce efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and review stored error codes for patterns. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • A water heater that has never been flushed or inspected
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Consider a water softener if hard water keeps building sediment and scale
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
