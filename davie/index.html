Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Davie

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

Common Water Heater Problems in Davie

In Davie, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
