Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Hollywood

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Water heaters in and around Hollywood deal with a predictable set of stresses. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be a real factor: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the popping and rumbling you may hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

Then there's age. Once the protective anode rod wears out, often well before the tank itself looks old, the steel starts to rust from the inside, and a drip from the bottom seam is usually the first visible sign. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Setup matters too. A home on a private well can see more sediment and occasionally a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater on hard water needs annual descaling. And an older installation being replaced may need code items it never had: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. All of these is fixable, and most are much cheaper when caught early.
