How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Start with the age. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. The symptoms come next. Water leaking from the tank, rust showing up only in the hot water, and heavy sediment noise all say the tank is close to finished. Parts like thermocouples, elements, and relief valves can be replaced on almost any heater, but a rusted-through tank cannot, and plenty of households in Plantation, FL usually find that out the hard way, with a wet floor.

A new water heater should fit the way your household uses hot water today, not the way it did when the old one went in. That is why Plantation Water Heater asks a few questions first: who showers when, whether there is a big soaking tub, and how many fixtures run at the same time. Tank heaters are compared by first-hour rating, while tankless units are sized by gallons per minute at the temperature rise needed in the coldest months. For a heat pump model, we check space and airflow, because it draws its heat from the surrounding room. We also confirm your gas line, vent, and electrical panel can handle the new unit, so there are no surprises on installation day.

The relief valve discharge is routed where it belongs, and we add an expansion tank, drain pan, or earthquake straps wherever local code requires them, including the permit if one applies. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area

Why Professional Water Heater Replacement Matters

Start with the venting. Gas water heater exhaust carries carbon monoxide, so an undersized, badly sloped, or loose vent can spill it back into living space. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and the details pile up quickly. Local code may also call for an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps, and some areas want a permit. A move to tankless or a heat pump brings gas line sizing, new vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines into the job.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, you will hear it from us directly.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Descale a tankless unit every year, or more often with hard water

A few tasks, like checking the temperature setting or lifting the relief valve lever to test it, are fine for homeowners, but most water heater work involves gas lines, venting, 240-volt circuits, or a tank full of scalding water, and that belongs with a professional.
